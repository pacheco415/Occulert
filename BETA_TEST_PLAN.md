# Occulert Beta Test Plan

Purpose: prove that Occulert can detect drowsiness reliably in real driving conditions before pitching fleets, investors, or partners.

## Test goals

- Reduce false alerts.
- Identify missed drowsiness events.
- Test phone mount positions.
- Test night driving and low-light conditions.
- Test glasses, sunglasses, and different face angles.
- Measure battery use and phone heat.
- Collect user feedback from real drivers.

## Recommended first beta group

Start with 5 to 10 trusted testers:

- Richard
- family/friends
- U-Haul coworkers if allowed by workplace rules
- delivery or rideshare drivers willing to test safely
- automotive classmates

## Safety rule

Do not intentionally drive while dangerously tired. Testing should be done safely, with a passenger when possible, and never as a reason to stay on the road when sleepy.

## Test scenarios

1. Daytime driving
2. Night driving
3. Sunglasses
4. Prescription glasses
5. Phone mounted high
6. Phone mounted low
7. Bumpy road
8. Highway speed
9. Stop-and-go traffic
10. Driver looking away briefly

## Data to record manually

For each drive, record:

- Date
- Phone model
- Browser
- Lighting condition
- Phone mount location
- Glasses/sunglasses used
- Number of alerts
- Any false alerts
- Any missed warnings
- Battery percentage before/after
- Phone heat level
- Notes from driver

## Simple scoring

Use a 1 to 5 score:

- 1 = not usable
- 2 = many issues
- 3 = usable but needs improvement
- 4 = good
- 5 = excellent

Score these categories:

- Detection accuracy
- Alert timing
- Ease of setup
- Battery impact
- Driver trust
- Overall usefulness

## Fleet pilot target

Once the app works well with 10 testers, prepare a small fleet pilot:

- 3 to 5 drivers
- 2 weeks
- daily feedback form
- summary report
- before/after safety insights
